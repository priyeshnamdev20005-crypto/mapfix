How to reply to a negative Google review

REVIEWS · 18 Sept 2026 · 8 MIN READ

A one-star review feels personal and the first instinct is to defend yourself. Resist it. The review is not a conversation with that customer — it is a piece of writing that every future customer will read before they decide whether to call you. Your reply is the only part of it you control.

In every listing audit we do, unanswered reviews are one of the most common findings, and the ones sitting there for two or three years do the most damage.

Why the reply matters more than the review

People expect a business to have a few bad reviews. A 4.6 with some criticism reads as real; a perfect 5.0 with twelve reviews reads as suspicious. What people actually judge you on is how you answered. A calm, specific reply under an angry review makes the angry review look like the exception. Silence makes it look true.

On one listing we audited, a customer had publicly offered to refer more work. The review sat unanswered for a year. That is not a review problem, it is a lost-business problem.

The four-part reply

  1. Thank them and name the problem. Show you read it. "Thank you for telling us — waiting forty minutes past your appointment time is not how we want to run the clinic."
  2. Apologise for the experience. You can be sorry someone had a bad time without admitting you were at fault. "I am sorry your visit went that way."
  3. Say what has changed. One concrete sentence. "We have cut Saturday bookings so appointments run on time." This is the line future customers actually care about.
  4. Move it off the page. "Please call me on 98xxx xxxxx and I will sort this out personally." Then do it.

Keep it to three or four sentences. Sign it with your name if you are the owner — it reads very differently from an anonymous reply.

Six things never to write

  • Never argue the facts in public. Even when you are right, the person reading it sees a business fighting a customer.
  • Never reveal personal details. No names, no order numbers, no medical or financial information. For clinics, salons, lawyers and anyone with a duty of confidentiality, do not even confirm that the person was a client.
  • Never blame the customer or your staff. "You arrived late" and "that was a new boy, he has been removed" both look bad.
  • Never paste the same reply under every review. Ten identical "Sorry for the inconvenience, please contact us" replies read like a bot.
  • Never offer money publicly. Say you want to put it right, and make the offer in private.
  • Never ask them in public to delete it. Solve the problem; if they want to update it, they will.

Ten replies you can copy

1. Long wait — clinic or salon
Thank you for telling us. Keeping you waiting that long is not acceptable and I am sorry. We have reduced the number of appointments in that slot so it does not happen again. Please call me on [number] — I would like to make your next visit right.

2. Work delivered late — interiors, tailoring, printing
You are right to be annoyed — we promised a date and we missed it. I am sorry. We have changed how we schedule jobs so we stop taking on more than we can finish on time. Please call me on [number] and I will personally see this through.

3. Food or product quality
Thank you for the honest feedback. That is not the standard we want to serve. I have spoken with the kitchen about it today. If you call me on [number] I would like to invite you back and show you the difference.

4. Staff behaviour
I am sorry — you should not have been spoken to that way, whatever the situation was. I have taken this up with the team myself. Please call me on [number]; I would like to hear what happened directly from you.

5. Price complaint
Thank you for the feedback. Our prices are on our website and I am sorry if they were not clear before the work started — that is on us. From now on we send a written estimate before we begin. If you feel you were charged for something you did not agree to, call me on [number] and I will look at it.

6. Nobody answered the phone / no-show
I am sorry, we let you down. We have moved our bookings to WhatsApp so nothing gets missed while we are on a job. Please message [number] and I will get someone to you this week.

7. One star, no text
I am sorry your experience was not good. We cannot see what went wrong from the rating alone — please call me on [number] or message us and I will try to put it right.

8. Review that appears to be about another business
Thank you for the review, but I think this may be meant for a different business — we have no record of this order. If we have made a mistake, please call me on [number] and I will look into it straight away.

9. Suspected competitor or fake review
We cannot find this booking in our records and we would genuinely like to help if we have missed something. Please call me on [number] with the date and I will check personally.

10. Tuition class or coaching — result complaint
Thank you for writing. I understand the disappointment and I am sorry. We are adding extra doubt sessions and a weekly progress update to parents so nobody is surprised at the end. Please call me on [number] — I would like to sit with you and go through it.

When a review really is fake

You can report a review from the profile: open the review, tap the three dots, and choose Report. Grounds that actually work are spam, off-topic content, conflict of interest (a competitor or an ex-employee), abusive language, and personal information. "Untrue" on its own is not grounds, and in our experience most honest reports come back rejected.

So report it once, then move on and write the calm reply. If a wave of fake one-stars arrives at the same time, report them together and use the Business Profile support chat — a pattern is easier for Google to see than a single review.

After the reply

The real fix for a bad review is more good ones burying it. Four new genuine reviews move a rating more than any argument will — that is the whole of this guide. Straight answers

Questions people ask about this

Can I get a bad Google review removed?

Only if it breaks Google’s rules — it is spam, it is about a different business, it contains abuse or personal information, or it comes from a competitor. "It is unfair" or "it is not true" is not grounds for removal, and most honest reports are rejected. Assume the review is staying and write a reply that future customers will read.

Should I reply to every review, even the good ones?

Yes. A profile where the owner answers everything reads as a business that is paying attention, and it is one of the things we check on every audit. Good reviews take one line; bad ones take four.

How quickly should I reply?

Within 24 hours if you can, and never in the first ten minutes while you are still annoyed. A reply written the next morning is almost always better than the one you would have sent last night.

Can I offer a refund or discount in the reply?

Do not put it in the public reply — it invites people to leave bad reviews for a discount. Say you would like to put it right and give a number or email; make the offer in private.

What if the review is from someone who was never a customer?

Report it, and reply anyway. Keep the reply calm and factual: say you cannot find them in your records, that you would like to look into it, and give a contact. Never state publicly that they are lying, and if you are a clinic or any business with a duty of confidentiality, never confirm or deny that a person visited you at all.
