How to get more Google reviews without annoying anyone

REVIEWS · 18 Sept 2026 · 6 MIN READ

Reviews are the one thing on your listing you cannot write yourself, and they are the thing customers look at first. A business with 40 reviews at 4.5 beats a business with 3 reviews at 5.0, every time.

The gap is almost never that customers are unhappy. It is that nobody asked them. In the six free checks we ran last month, every single business had fewer reviews than the competitor ranked above it, and none of them had a system for asking.

Step 1 — Get your review link

Sign in with the Google account that owns the profile, search your business name on Google, and find Ask for reviews in the profile panel. Google gives you a short link that opens the review box directly, already on the right business.

Save that link somewhere you can reach in two seconds — pinned in WhatsApp, in your phone’s notes, in your email signature. If you have to hunt for it, you will stop asking.

Step 2 — Put it on a card at the counter

Turn the link into a QR code (any free generator works) and print it on a small standee or card: the QR, four words of instruction, and nothing else. It costs a few hundred rupees at a local printer. For salons, clinics, restaurants and shops this quietly brings in reviews from people you never got round to asking.

Step 3 — Ask at the right moment

The right moment is the moment the customer is happiest, which is almost always right at the end — the haircut is done and they like it, the AC is working again, the design has been approved. Not three days later.

Ask out loud first, then send the link. The spoken ask is what makes people do it; the link just removes the effort.

"I am glad you are happy with it. Could you put that in a Google review? It genuinely helps a small business like ours. I will send you the link right now on WhatsApp."

Step 4 — The message that actually gets a reply

Short, personal, one link, no marketing. Send it within an hour of the job, while they still remember you.

WhatsApp:
Hi [name], thank you for coming in today. If you have a minute, a quick Google review would really help us — it takes about thirty seconds.
[link]
— [your name], [business name]

One follow-up after three days is fine if they did not reply. A second follow-up is pestering; stop there.

Step 5 — Reply to every one

Answer the good ones in a line and the bad ones properly — the method is in our guide on replying to negative reviews. A visible owner reply encourages the next person to write one, because they can see somebody reads them.

What you are not allowed to do

  • No incentives. No discount, free dessert, lucky draw or cashback in exchange for a review. This is the rule most Indian businesses break without realising it.
  • No gating. Do not screen people first and send the link only to the happy ones.
  • No buying reviews. The accounts get detected, the reviews get wiped, and the profile can be suspended.
  • No reviews from staff, family or your own second account. Google is good at spotting them and they drag the whole listing down.
  • No bulk blast. Thirty reviews appearing in two days on a listing that had four looks exactly like what it is.

A realistic pace

Ask ten customers a week and you will get two or three reviews a week — over a hundred in a year, arriving steadily, which is also what Google prefers to see. That is enough to overtake almost any local competitor, and it costs nothing.

If the reviews are coming but the rest of the profile is empty — no services, wrong category, no photos — they will not turn into calls. Straight answers

Questions people ask about this

Can I give a discount or a gift for a Google review?

No. Offering anything of value in exchange for a review breaks Google’s policy, and reviews bought that way get removed in batches along with the good ones you earned honestly. You can thank someone warmly; you cannot pay them.

Is it against the rules to ask only happy customers?

Yes. Filtering people — asking how the visit went and only sending the link to those who say "good" — is called review gating and Google prohibits it. Send the link to everyone and let the ratings fall where they fall.

How many people actually leave a review when asked?

Roughly one in four or five if you ask in person and follow up once with a direct link. Sending a bare link with no conversation converts far worse. Ten genuine asks a week is a realistic target for most small businesses.

Can I buy Google reviews?

You can, and it is a bad idea. Bought reviews come from accounts that review dozens of unrelated businesses, Google removes them in sweeps, and a listing that loses thirty reviews overnight looks worse than one that never had them. It can also get the profile suspended.

Where do I find my Google review link?

Open your Business Profile on Google — search your business name while signed in as the owner — and look for "Ask for reviews". It gives you a short g.page link you can paste into WhatsApp or turn into a QR code.
